    How the model works
    • Each pipe is a column of water following the unsteady Bernoulli equation: pressure, gravity, the water's inertia, and pipe friction.
    • The screw turbine carries a fixed volume of water per turn, plus some leakage past the blades. The pressure drop across it is what turns the shaft.
    • The Pelton wheel is driven by a free jet: the nozzle turns the water's height into speed, and each bucket takes momentum from the jet. Its torque is ρ·Q·(jet speed − bucket speed)·1.8·r, so it can capture at most 90% of the jet's energy, and only when the buckets move at half the jet's speed. Its casing must stay at air pressure, so the water then drains to the lower tank by gravity.
    • The generator brakes the shaft with a torque proportional to speed. The braking power is the electricity.
    • The weight is a sealed piston. It adds its weight ÷ area to the pressure of the water underneath. Water that lands on top of it adds to that weight.
    • A sealed big vessel traps air that obeys Boyle's law (pressure × volume stays constant). When its pressure drops below the outside air, that is the "partial vacuum" from the docs.
    • The one-way valves open above their opening pressure and shut as soon as flow tries to reverse.
    • Water falling from the spout, and over the top of the return pipe, loses the energy of its fall when it splashes.
    • The air-release tube is drawn as a pressure gauge: its water level shows the pressure at the turbine inlet. It empties when that pressure falls below the outside air.
    • Pipes are assumed to stay full, with no air leaking in. That is the most favourable assumption for the design.
    • Every loss is calculated directly from the flows, never as a leftover. That makes "electricity + losses = released energy" an independent check on the arithmetic.
